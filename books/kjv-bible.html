KJV Study Notes

Background from that page: authorized 1604 by King James I, finished 1611, 85 years after Tyndale's 1526 New Testament. BibleGateway text matches the 1987 printing. Public domain in the United States.

Canon used for this study

66 books. 39 Old Testament, 27 New Testament.

Reading rule for answers

  • Answer from the KJV wording, not a modern paraphrase, unless the reader asks for a paraphrase.
  • Give book, chapter, and verse for claims that depend on a passage.
  • When a question turns on a single verse or a disputed reading, re-check the KJV text on BibleGateway before answering, rather than relying on memory alone.
  • Distinguish what the text says, what is a common interpretation, and what is uncertain.
  • Note KJV-specific language where it matters: thee/thou/ye, verily, charity for love in 1 Corinthians 13, and other early modern usages that change in modern translations.

KJV language to watch

  • Thou/thee/thy: singular you. Ye/you: plural you in most KJV usage. This matters in passages like John 3:7, where "ye" is plural.
  • Verily: truly, amen, used to mark a solemn statement, especially in Jesus' teaching.
  • Charity: in 1 Corinthians 13 and elsewhere, the KJV word for love as active, self giving love, not just feeling.
  • Repent: a change of mind and direction, not only regret.
  • Righteousness: right standing and right conduct before God, not only moral niceness.
  • Covenant: a binding agreement with promises, signs, and obligations, central to the whole story.
  • Propitiation: in Romans 3:25 and 1 John 2:2, the atoning sacrifice that turns away wrath.
  • Conversation in KJV often means conduct or way of life, not just speech. Example: Philippians 1:27, 1 Peter 1:15.
  • Prevent in KJV often means go before, not stop. Example: 1 Thessalonians 4:15.

Story arc in one sweep

1. Creation and fall: Genesis 1-3. God creates, humanity is given stewardship, disobedience brings death and exile from Eden.

2. Promise to Abraham: Genesis 12, 15, 17. Land, descendants, blessing to all nations.

3. Exodus and law: Exodus to Deuteronomy. Deliverance from Egypt, Sinai covenant, tabernacle, wilderness testing.

4. Conquest and judges: Joshua to Ruth. Settlement, cycles of disobedience and deliverance.

5. United and divided kingdom: Samuel to Kings and Chronicles. Davidic covenant in 2 Samuel 7, temple, division, exile.

6. Exile and return: Prophets, Ezra, Nehemiah, Esther. Judgment, remnant, rebuilding.

7. Wisdom in the meantime: Job, Psalms, Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, Song of Solomon. Suffering, prayer, practical wisdom, limits of human wisdom.

8. Jesus: Gospels. Birth, teaching, miracles, death, resurrection. Matthew presents him as king and fulfillment, Mark as servant in action, Luke as savior for all people, John as the Word made flesh.

9. Church: Acts. Pentecost, mission to Jews and Gentiles, Paul's journeys.

10. Teaching for the church: Epistles. Doctrine, conduct, correction, endurance.

11. End: Revelation. Judgment, new heaven and new earth, Revelation 21-22.

Book by book outline

Law

  • Genesis: Creation, patriarchs, Joseph. Key: Genesis 1, 3, 12, 15, 22, 37-50.
  • Exodus: Deliverance, Passover, Sinai, tabernacle. Key: Exodus 3, 12, 14, 20, 32-34.
  • Leviticus: Holiness, sacrifice, priesthood, feasts. Key: Leviticus 16, 19, 23.
  • Numbers: Wilderness census, rebellion, Balaam, second generation. Key: Numbers 13-14, 21.
  • Deuteronomy: Second law, covenant renewal before entry. Key: Deuteronomy 6, 28, 30.

History

  • Joshua: Conquest and division of the land. Key: Joshua 1, 6, 24.
  • Judges: Cycles of apostasy, oppression, deliverer. Key: Judges 2, 6-8, 13-16.
  • Ruth: Loyalty, redemption, ancestor of David. Key: Ruth 1, 4.
  • 1 Samuel: Samuel, Saul, David anointed. Key: 1 Samuel 8, 15-17, 31.
  • 2 Samuel: David's reign, covenant, failure, consequences. Key: 2 Samuel 7, 11-12.
  • 1 Kings: Solomon, temple, division. Key: 1 Kings 3, 8, 11-12, 17-19.
  • 2 Kings: Two kingdoms, fall of Israel 722 BC, fall of Judah 586 BC. Key: 2 Kings 17, 25.
  • 1 Chronicles: Genealogy to David, temple preparation, retold with priestly focus.
  • 2 Chronicles: Solomon to exile, focus on temple and reforming kings.
  • Ezra: Return, temple rebuilt, reform.
  • Nehemiah: Wall rebuilt, covenant renewal.
  • Esther: Providence in Persia, deliverance of the Jews, Purim.

Wisdom and poetry

  • Job: Suffering, God's speeches, trust without full explanation. Key: Job 1-2, 38-42.
  • Psalms: 150 songs and prayers. Lament, praise, royal psalms, wisdom psalms. Key: Psalms 1, 19, 22, 23, 51, 110, 119.
  • Proverbs: Practical wisdom, fear of the Lord as beginning of wisdom, Proverbs 9:10. Key: Proverbs 3, 8, 31.
  • Ecclesiastes: Vanity of life under the sun, fear God and keep his commandments, Ecclesiastes 12:13.
  • Song of Solomon: Love poetry, read literally as marital love and, in tradition, as an image of God's love for his people.

Major prophets

  • Isaiah: Judgment and comfort, Servant Songs, new heavens and new earth. Key: Isaiah 6, 9, 40, 53, 61, 65-66.
  • Jeremiah: Weeping prophet, new covenant promise, Jeremiah 31:31-34.
  • Lamentations: Laments over fallen Jerusalem.
  • Ezekiel: Visions, valley of dry bones, new heart, Ezekiel 36-37.
  • Daniel: Exile faith, apocalyptic visions, Son of man, Daniel 7.

Minor prophets

  • Hosea: Unfaithful Israel as unfaithful wife, redeeming love.
  • Joel: Locust plague, day of the Lord, outpouring of Spirit, Joel 2:28.
  • Amos: Justice, "let judgment run down as waters," Amos 5:24.
  • Obadiah: Judgment on Edom.
  • Jonah: Mercy to Nineveh, prophet's resistance.
  • Micah: Justice, mercy, humility, Micah 6:8.
  • Nahum: Fall of Nineveh.
  • Habakkuk: Why the wicked prosper, faith, Habakkuk 2:4.
  • Zephaniah: Day of the Lord, remnant.
  • Haggai: Rebuild the temple after return.
  • Zechariah: Visions, coming king, Zechariah 9:9.
  • Malachi: Final prophet, covenant failure, coming messenger, Malachi 3:1, 4:5.

Gospels

  • Matthew: Jesus as Messiah and king, Sermon on the Mount Matthew 5-7, Great Commission Matthew 28:18-20.
  • Mark: Shortest, urgent, action focused, suffering servant, Mark 10:45.
  • Luke: Orderly account, emphasis on prayer, poor, outsiders, parables of lost things Luke 15.
  • John: Word, signs, "I am" sayings, upper room discourse, resurrection. Key: John 1, 3, 6, 10, 14-17, 20.

Acts

  • Acts: Ascension, Pentecost Acts 2, Stephen, Philip, Cornelius, Paul's conversion Acts 9, missionary journeys, journey to Rome. Bridge from Gospels to Epistles.

Pauline epistles

  • Romans: Sin, justification by faith, life in the Spirit, Israel, practical conduct. Key: Romans 3, 5, 8, 12.
  • 1 Corinthians: Division, gifts, love chapter 13, resurrection chapter 15.
  • 2 Corinthians: Ministry, weakness, reconciliation, generosity.
  • Galatians: Justification by faith, not law, freedom in Christ.
  • Ephesians: Unity in Christ, church as body, armor of God chapter 6.
  • Philippians: Joy, humility of Christ chapter 2, contentment.
  • Colossians: Supremacy of Christ, chapter 1.
  • 1 Thessalonians: Second coming, holy living.
  • 2 Thessalonians: Day of the Lord, correction of confusion.
  • 1 Timothy: Church order, sound doctrine, leadership.
  • 2 Timothy: Paul's last charge, endure, Scripture, 2 Timothy 3:16.
  • Titus: Sound doctrine and good works in Crete.
  • Philemon: Onesimus, forgiveness, brotherhood over status.

General epistles

  • Hebrews: Christ greater than angels, Moses, priesthood, new covenant, faith chapter 11.
  • James: Faith shown by works, tongue, wisdom, care for the poor.
  • 1 Peter: Suffering, holy living, hope in exile.
  • 2 Peter: False teachers, certainty of Christ's return.
  • 1 John: Love, truth, assurance, God is love, 1 John 4:8.
  • 2 John: Truth and love, warning against deceivers.
  • 3 John: Hospitality and truth in practice.
  • Jude: Contend for the faith, warning against ungodly teachers.

Revelation

  • Revelation: Letters to seven churches chapters 2-3, seals, trumpets, bowls, Babylon, return of Christ chapter 19, judgment chapter 20, new heaven and new earth chapters 21-22. Highly symbolic; read with Old Testament images in mind, especially Daniel, Ezekiel, and Isaiah.

Core themes to track

  • Covenant: Noah Genesis 9, Abraham Genesis 15 and 17, Moses Exodus 19-24, David 2 Samuel 7, new covenant Jeremiah 31 and Luke 22:20.
  • Sacrifice and atonement: Passover Exodus 12, Day of Atonement Leviticus 16, Servant Isaiah 53, cross in the Gospels, Hebrews 9-10.
  • Kingdom: Present in Jesus' teaching, future in Revelation, tension held throughout the New Testament.
  • Law and grace: Law reveals sin, Romans 3:20, grace saves through faith, Ephesians 2:8-9, law fulfilled not discarded, Matthew 5:17.
  • Wisdom: Fear of the Lord, Proverbs 9:10, tested in Job and Ecclesiastes, fulfilled in Christ, 1 Corinthians 1:24, 30.
  • Judgment and mercy: Held together in the prophets, in the cross, and in Revelation.

Cross references that unlock a lot

  • Genesis 3:15 to Revelation 12: seed of the woman.
  • Isaiah 53 to the passion accounts and Acts 8:32-35.
  • Psalm 22 to the crucifixion accounts.
  • Psalm 110 to Hebrews and the Gospels' use of it.
  • Daniel 7:13-14 to Jesus' Son of man sayings.
  • Joel 2:28-32 to Acts 2.
  • Jeremiah 31:31-34 to Hebrews 8.

Reading log and detailed notes: Pentateuch

Genesis 1, rechecked verbatim Nov 3, 2026 on BibleGateway KJV

  • Opening line: "In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth." Genesis 1:1.
  • Pattern: God speaks, it happens, God sees it is good, evening and morning mark a day.
  • Humanity is distinct: made in God's image, male and female, given dominion and the command to be fruitful and multiply, Genesis 1:26-28.
  • Note the KJV plural in 1:26, "Let us make man," which later readers connect to the plurality in the Godhead, while the text itself does not explain it there. Keep that distinction in answers.

Genesis, chapter groups

  • Genesis 1-11: Primeval history. Creation, fall, Cain and Abel, flood, Babel. Covenant with Noah, Genesis 9, sign of the rainbow.
  • Genesis 12-25: Abraham. Call Genesis 12:1-3, covenant Genesis 15, circumcision Genesis 17, binding of Isaac Genesis 22.
  • Genesis 26-36: Isaac and Jacob. Jacob's ladder Genesis 28, wrestling Genesis 32, twelve sons.
  • Genesis 37-50: Joseph. Dreams, betrayal, Egypt, reconciliation, "ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good," Genesis 50:20.

Exodus, chapter groups

  • Exodus 1-15: Oppression, Moses' call in Exodus 3, plagues, Passover Exodus 12, Red Sea Exodus 14, Song of Moses Exodus 15.
  • Exodus 16-18: Wilderness provision: manna, water, Jethro's counsel.
  • Exodus 19-24: Sinai covenant, Ten Commandments Exodus 20, covenant ratified Exodus 24.
  • Exodus 25-31: Tabernacle instructions.
  • Exodus 32-34: Golden calf, intercession, second tablets, God's name proclaimed Exodus 34:6-7.
  • Exodus 35-40: Tabernacle built and filled with glory.

Leviticus, chapter groups

  • Leviticus 1-7: Offerings: burnt, meat, peace, sin, trespass.
  • Leviticus 8-10: Priesthood ordained, Nadab and Abihu.
  • Leviticus 11-15: Clean and unclean, holiness in daily life.
  • Leviticus 16: Day of Atonement, central to the book.
  • Leviticus 17-26: Holiness Code, "ye shall be holy: for I the LORD your God am holy," Leviticus 19:2, feasts Leviticus 23, jubilee Leviticus 25.
  • Leviticus 27: Vows and redemption.

Numbers, chapter groups

  • Numbers 1-10: Census, camp order, departure from Sinai.
  • Numbers 11-21: Complaint, spies and 40 years, Numbers 13-14, Korah's rebellion, Aaron's rod, bronze serpent Numbers 21:9, later used in John 3:14.
  • Numbers 22-25: Balaam, blessing instead of curse.
  • Numbers 26-36: Second census, inheritance, cities of refuge.

Deuteronomy, chapter groups

  • Deuteronomy 1-4: Review of the journey, warning against idolatry.
  • Deuteronomy 5-11: Ten Commandments restated, Shema Deuteronomy 6:4-5, "love the LORD thy God with all thine heart."
  • Deuteronomy 12-26: Laws for life in the land.
  • Deuteronomy 27-30: Blessings and curses, choose life, Deuteronomy 30:19.
  • Deuteronomy 31-34: Joshua commissioned, Song of Moses, blessing, death of Moses.

Pentateuch questions to be ready for

  • Why does Genesis give two creation accounts, Genesis 1 and Genesis 2, and how do they fit?
  • What is a covenant, and how do the Noah, Abraham, and Sinai covenants differ?
  • How does Passover in Exodus 12 become a pattern for later redemption language?
  • What does holiness mean in Leviticus, and how does Hebrews reframe sacrifice?
  • Why 40 years in the wilderness, and what changes between the two censuses in Numbers?

Reading log and detailed notes: Historical books

Joshua

  • Joshua 1: Commission, "be strong and of a good courage," law as the guide for success.
  • Joshua 2-6: Rahab, Jordan crossing, Jericho. Faith and obedience, not just military power.
  • Joshua 7-12: Achan, Ai, campaigns, Gibeon.
  • Joshua 13-21: Division of the land by tribe, cities of refuge.
  • Joshua 23-24: Farewell, covenant renewal at Shechem, "choose you this day whom ye will serve," Joshua 24:15.

Judges

  • Judges 2: Thesis for the book. After Joshua's generation, Israel forgets, serves Baal, is oppressed, cries out, is delivered.
  • Pattern repeats: sin, servitude, supplication, salvation, rest, relapse.
  • Judges 4-5: Deborah and Barak, song of victory.
  • Judges 6-8: Gideon, reduction of the army, victory by God's power, then the ephod trap.
  • Judges 13-16: Samson. Strength tied to Nazirite vow, flawed deliverer, final act against the Philistines.
  • Judges 17-21: No king in Israel, "every man did that which was right in his own eyes," Judges 21:25. Sets up the need for a king.

Ruth

  • Ruth 1: Naomi, Orpah, Ruth's loyalty, "whither thou goest, I will go," Ruth 1:16.
  • Ruth 2: Boaz, gleaning, kindness to a foreigner.
  • Ruth 3: Threshing floor, proposal in the language of redemption.
  • Ruth 4: Kinsman redeemer, marriage, Obed, line to David. Providence in ordinary loyalty.

1 Samuel

  • 1 Samuel 1-3: Hannah, Samuel called.
  • 1 Samuel 4-7: Ark captured and returned, Ebenezer.
  • 1 Samuel 8: Israel asks for a king, "that we also may be like all the nations."
  • 1 Samuel 9-15: Saul anointed, early success, disobedience, rejection. "To obey is better than sacrifice," 1 Samuel 15:22.
  • 1 Samuel 16-31: David anointed, Goliath chapter 17, friendship with Jonathan, flight from Saul, Saul's death.

2 Samuel

  • 2 Samuel 1-4: David becomes king over Judah, then all Israel.
  • 2 Samuel 5-10: Jerusalem, ark brought up, victories.
  • 2 Samuel 7: Davidic covenant. God promises David a house and a throne forever, the key promise behind later messianic expectation.
  • 2 Samuel 11-12: Bathsheba, Uriah, Nathan's confrontation, repentance in Psalm 51.
  • 2 Samuel 13-20: Family rebellion, Absalom, consequences of sin in the house.
  • 2 Samuel 21-24: Final acts, census, plague, altar on Araunah's threshing floor.

1 and 2 Kings

  • 1 Kings 1-11: Solomon, wisdom chapter 3, temple chapter 8, wealth, foreign wives, division after his death.
  • 1 Kings 12-22: Rehoboam and Jeroboam, divided kingdom, Elijah, Ahab and Jezebel, Naboth, Micaiah.
  • 2 Kings 1-13: Elisha, Naaman chapter 5, Jehu, Athaliah, Joash.
  • 2 Kings 14-17: Decline, fall of Israel to Assyria in 722 BC, reason given in chapter 17: idolatry and breaking covenant.
  • 2 Kings 18-25: Hezekiah's reform, Josiah's reform chapter 22-23, fall of Judah to Babylon in 586 BC, chapter 25.

1 and 2 Chronicles

  • Retelling of the same history with a priestly and temple focus. Genealogies 1 Chronicles 1-9, David's reign, Solomon's temple, then Judah's kings in 2 Chronicles, ending with Cyrus' decree to rebuild, which points forward to Ezra.

Ezra, Nehemiah, Esther

  • Ezra 1-6: Return under Cyrus, temple rebuilt despite opposition.
  • Ezra 7-10: Ezra's reform, covenant faithfulness, separation from foreign marriages.
  • Nehemiah 1-6: Wall rebuilt in 52 days, opposition met with prayer and work.
  • Nehemiah 7-13: Census, law read, covenant renewal, reforms on Sabbath, tithes, marriage.
  • Esther: No direct mention of God by name in the book, yet providence drives the plot. Esther 4:14, "for such a time as this," Haman's fall, Purim, deliverance without a miracle on the surface.

Historical books questions to be ready for

  • Why does Judges repeat its cycle, and what does it say about leadership without a king?
  • How does the Davidic covenant in 2 Samuel 7 shape the rest of the Old Testament and the Gospels?
  • Why do Kings and Chronicles tell the same story differently, and what does each emphasize?
  • How does Esther show providence when God is not named?

Reading log and detailed notes: Wisdom and Prophets

Job

  • Job 1-2: Satan's challenge, Job loses wealth, children, health, yet does not curse God.
  • Job 3-31: Debates with Eliphaz, Bildad, Zophar. Friends assume suffering equals guilt. Job insists on his integrity and demands a hearing.
  • Job 32-37: Elihu, anger and a different angle, God is just and speaks in many ways.
  • Job 38-41: God's speeches from the whirlwind. Creation questions Job cannot answer. The point is not an explanation of suffering, it is God's wisdom and power.
  • Job 42: Job repents of speaking beyond knowledge, prays for his friends, is restored. The book refuses a simple formula.

Psalms

  • Structure helps: Books 1 to 5, Psalms 1-41, 42-72, 73-89, 90-106, 107-150.
  • Psalm 1: Two ways, righteous and wicked, law as delight.
  • Psalm 19: God's glory in creation and in the law.
  • Psalm 22: Suffering and abandonment, quoted at the cross, ending in praise and a worldwide kingdom.
  • Psalm 23: Shepherd, provision, presence in the valley.
  • Psalm 51: Repentance after David's sin, "create in me a clean heart."
  • Psalm 110: Priest king, most quoted Old Testament passage in the New Testament.
  • Psalm 119: Longest psalm, love of the law in every stanza.
  • Read laments as prayers, not just poems. Many move from complaint to trust without solving the problem.

Proverbs

  • Proverbs 1-9: Wisdom personified, calling in the streets, warning against folly and adultery.
  • Proverbs 10-29: Short sayings, mostly contrasts. Diligence versus sloth, truthful speech, discipline, generosity, humility.
  • Proverbs 30: Agur, limits of human knowledge.
  • Proverbs 31: Virtuous woman, diligence, fear of the Lord as the praise that lasts, verse 30.
  • Proverbs are general principles, not unconditional promises. Read them that way, or Job and Ecclesiastes will correct the misuse.

Ecclesiastes

  • Ecclesiastes 1-2: Vanity under the sun, pleasure, work, wisdom all fail to satisfy on their own.
  • Ecclesiastes 3: Time for everything, eternity set in the heart, verse 11.
  • Ecclesiastes 5-11: Limits, injustice, wealth, death, enjoy the good given by God without making it an idol.
  • Ecclesiastes 12: Remember the Creator in youth, conclusion, "fear God, and keep his commandments: for this is the whole duty of man," verse 13.

Song of Solomon

  • Chapters 1-8: Love, longing, marriage, separation and reunion. Read as celebration of marital love first. Allegorical readings are later tradition, not the plain sense of the poetry, and answers should say which level is being used.

Isaiah

  • Isaiah 1-12: Judgment on Judah, Immanuel sign chapter 7, Prince of Peace chapter 9, Branch chapter 11.
  • Isaiah 13-27: Judgments on nations, apocalyptic hope.
  • Isaiah 28-39: Trust in Assyria or Egypt versus trust in God, Hezekiah, deliverance of Jerusalem.
  • Isaiah 40-55: Comfort, Servant Songs, especially chapter 53, suffering servant, atonement, exaltation.
  • Isaiah 56-66: New community, new heavens and new earth, chapter 65-66.

Jeremiah and Lamentations

  • Jeremiah 1: Call, "before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee."
  • Jeremiah 7: Temple sermon, trust in ritual without obedience is false.
  • Jeremiah 20: Cost of prophecy, complaint.
  • Jeremiah 29: Letter to exiles, seek the welfare of the city, verse 7, plans for hope, verse 11, in context of 70 years.
  • Jeremiah 31: New covenant, law written on hearts, forgiveness.
  • Jeremiah 36: Scroll burned, word survives.
  • Lamentations 1-5: Five laments over Jerusalem, mercy in the middle, chapter 3:22-23, "his mercies... are new every morning."

Ezekiel

  • Ezekiel 1: Vision of God's glory, wheels and creatures, hard to picture, meant to overwhelm.
  • Ezekiel 3: Watchman responsibility.
  • Ezekiel 8-11: Glory departs the temple.
  • Ezekiel 16 and 23: Jerusalem as unfaithful wife, harsh images that need careful, non sensational handling.
  • Ezekiel 34: Shepherds condemned, God as shepherd, Davidic shepherd to come.
  • Ezekiel 36: New heart and new spirit, verses 26-27.
  • Ezekiel 37: Valley of dry bones, national restoration.
  • Ezekiel 40-48: New temple vision, restoration order.

Daniel

  • Daniel 1-6: Faith in exile, food, furnace, lions, "we will not serve thy gods," chapter 3:18.
  • Daniel 2: Statue, kingdoms, stone not cut by hands.
  • Daniel 7: Four beasts, Son of man given dominion, verses 13-14, source of Jesus' title.
  • Daniel 9: Prayer, seventy weeks, difficult chronology, answers should flag uncertainty rather than force a date.
  • Daniel 12: Resurrection language, "many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake," verse 2.

Minor Prophets, in brief

  • Hosea: Marriage to Gomer as a living picture of Israel's unfaithfulness and God's redeeming love, Hosea 3.
  • Joel: Locusts, day of the Lord, Spirit poured out, Joel 2:28, quoted in Acts 2.
  • Amos: Justice for the poor, worship without justice is rejected, Amos 5:21-24.
  • Obadiah: Edom judged for rejoicing over Judah's fall.
  • Jonah: Prophet flees mercy, Nineveh repents, Jonah resents grace, chapter 4 question left open.
  • Micah: Bethlehem ruler chapter 5:2, justice and mercy chapter 6:8, God casting sins into the sea chapter 7:19.
  • Nahum: Nineveh falls, God's vengeance and goodness held together, chapter 1:7.
  • Habakkuk: Complaint, God's answer, "the just shall live by his faith," chapter 2:4, quoted in Romans and Galatians.
  • Zephaniah: Day of the Lord, remnant, singing God, chapter 3:17.
  • Haggai: Rebuild after return, priorities, "consider your ways," chapter 1:7.
  • Zechariah: Eight night visions, crowning, coming king on a donkey chapter 9:9, shepherd struck chapter 13:7.
  • Malachi: Final Old Testament voice, robbing God, refining, Elijah before the great day, chapter 4:5, bridge to John the Baptist.

Wisdom and Prophets questions to be ready for

  • Why does Job not get an explanation, and what does that do to a simple retribution theology?
  • How do Proverbs, Job, and Ecclesiastes correct each other?
  • Which Servant Songs in Isaiah are about Israel, which about an individual, and where do they overlap?
  • How does the new covenant in Jeremiah 31 differ from Sinai, and what stays the same?

Reading log and detailed notes: New Testament

Matthew

  • Matthew 1-4: Genealogy to Abraham and David, birth, baptism, temptation.
  • Matthew 5-7: Sermon on the Mount. Beatitudes, law fulfilled, prayer, treasures, narrow gate.
  • Matthew 8-12: Miracles, authority, opposition grows.
  • Matthew 13: Kingdom parables, especially the sower.
  • Matthew 14-20: Feeding, confession at Caesarea Philippi chapter 16, transfiguration chapter 17, teaching on forgiveness and greatness.
  • Matthew 21-25: Entry, temple, woes, Olivet discourse.
  • Matthew 26-28: Passion, resurrection, Great Commission chapter 28:18-20, "all power is given unto me in heaven and in earth."

Mark

  • Mark 1-3: Immediate action, "straightway" pace, authority in teaching and healing.
  • Mark 4-8: Parables, miracles, feeding, Peter's confession, first passion prediction.
  • Mark 8-10: Discipleship as following a suffering Messiah, "whosoever will come after me, let him deny himself," chapter 8:34, ransom saying chapter 10:45.
  • Mark 11-16: Entry, temple, passion, empty tomb. Note the KJV ending at chapter 16:20 includes the longer ending, verses 9-20, whose textual history should be flagged if a question turns on it.

Luke

  • Luke 1-2: Birth narratives, Magnificat, Benedictus, shepherds, Simeon and Anna.
  • Luke 3-4: Baptism, genealogy to Adam, temptation.
  • Luke 5-9: Call, miracles, Sermon on the Plain, sending of the twelve and seventy.
  • Luke 10-19: Travel narrative, parables of the good Samaritan chapter 10, prodigal son chapter 15, rich man and Lazarus chapter 16, Zacchaeus chapter 19.
  • Luke 20-24: Passion, Emmaus road chapter 24, ascension. Luke pairs with Acts as one story.

John

  • John 1: Word made flesh, "In the beginning was the Word," verse 1, "the Word was made flesh," verse 14.
  • John 2-4: Signs, temple, Nicodemus chapter 3, "ye must be born again," verse 7, Samaritan woman chapter 4.
  • John 5-10: Sabbath healing, bread of life chapter 6, light of the world chapter 8, good shepherd chapter 10, "I and my Father are one," chapter 10:30.
  • John 11-12: Lazarus, anointing, entry.
  • John 13-17: Foot washing, farewell discourse, "I am the way, the truth, and the life," chapter 14:6, high priestly prayer chapter 17.
  • John 18-21: Passion, resurrection, Thomas, restoration of Peter, "feed my sheep," chapter 21:17.

Acts

  • Acts 1-2: Ascension, Pentecost, Peter's sermon, church formed.
  • Acts 3-7: Healing, persecution, Stephen's speech and death.
  • Acts 8-12: Samaria, Ethiopian eunuch, Saul's conversion chapter 9, Cornelius chapter 10, Gentile inclusion, Peter freed.
  • Acts 13-20: Paul's journeys, Jerusalem council chapter 15, key decision that Gentiles need not be circumcised.
  • Acts 21-28: Paul's arrest, trials, shipwreck, Rome, gospel preached "with all confidence, no man forbidding him," chapter 28:31.

Romans

  • Romans 1-3: All under sin, Jew and Gentile, justification by faith, chapter 3:28.
  • Romans 4-5: Abraham as example of faith, peace and hope through Christ.
  • Romans 6-8: Dead to sin, alive to God, law and Spirit, "there is therefore now no condemnation," chapter 8:1, groaning creation, chapter 8:18-25.
  • Romans 9-11: Israel, election, remnant, grafting, mystery, chapter 11:25.
  • Romans 12-16: Living sacrifice, gifts, love, government, weak and strong, greetings.

1 and 2 Corinthians

  • 1 Corinthians 1-4: Divisions, wisdom of the cross, "Christ crucified," chapter 1:23.
  • 1 Corinthians 5-7: Discipline, lawsuits, marriage, body as temple.
  • 1 Corinthians 8-10: Food offered to idols, freedom limited by love.
  • 1 Corinthians 11-14: Worship order, Lord's Supper, spiritual gifts, love chapter 13, tongues and prophecy.
  • 1 Corinthians 15: Resurrection, core gospel verses 3-4, order of resurrection, victory over death.
  • 2 Corinthians 1-7: Comfort in suffering, ministry of reconciliation, chapter 5:17-21.
  • 2 Corinthians 8-9: Generosity, cheerful giver, chapter 9:7.
  • 2 Corinthians 10-13: Paul defends his apostleship, thorn in the flesh chapter 12, strength in weakness.

Galatians to Colossians

  • Galatians: No other gospel chapter 1, justification by faith chapter 2:16, law as schoolmaster chapter 3:24, fruit of the Spirit chapter 5:22-23.
  • Ephesians: Chosen and blessed chapter 1, saved by grace through faith chapter 2:8-9, one body chapter 4, household chapter 5-6, armor chapter 6:10-18.
  • Philippians: Joy in prison, Christ hymn chapter 2:5-11, "I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me," chapter 4:13, in context of contentment.
  • Colossians: Christ supreme chapter 1:15-20, fullness, warning against hollow philosophy chapter 2:8.

Thessalonians to Philemon

  • 1 Thessalonians: Faith, love, hope, second coming chapter 4:13-18, "comfort one another with these words."
  • 2 Thessalonians: Day of the Lord, man of sin chapter 2, work ethic chapter 3:10.
  • 1 Timothy: Sound doctrine, prayer, leadership qualifications chapter 3, godliness with contentment chapter 6:6.
  • 2 Timothy: Last charge, "preach the word," chapter 4:2, Scripture inspired chapter 3:16, crown of righteousness chapter 4:8.
  • Titus: Order in Crete, sound doctrine, good works, regeneration chapter 3:5.
  • Philemon: Onesimus returned, appeal in love, equality in Christ without directly attacking the institution in the letter.

Hebrews to Jude

  • Hebrews: Christ greater than angels chapter 1, than Moses chapter 3, than Aaron chapter 7, new covenant chapter 8, once for all sacrifice chapter 10, faith chapter 11, discipline chapter 12.
  • James: Faith and works chapter 2, tongue chapter 3, wisdom from above chapter 3:17, prayer chapter 5.
  • 1 Peter: Living hope, suffering, holy nation chapter 2:9, submission, shepherds chapter 5.
  • 2 Peter: Growth chapter 1, false teachers chapter 2, day of the Lord chapter 3, "one day is with the Lord as a thousand years," verse 8.
  • 1 John: Fellowship, light, love, assurance, tests of true faith, "God is love," chapter 4:8.
  • 2 John: Walk in truth, do not receive deceivers.
  • 3 John: Gaius commended, Diotrephes warned, Demetrius praised.
  • Jude: Contend for the faith, examples of rebellion, doxology verses 24-25.

Revelation

  • Revelation 1: Vision of the risen Christ, letters commanded.
  • Revelation 2-3: Seven churches, each with commendation, rebuke, promise to the overcomer.
  • Revelation 4-5: Throne, Lamb worthy to open the scroll.
  • Revelation 6-11: Seals and trumpets, witnesses, judgment increasing.
  • Revelation 12-14: Woman, dragon, beasts, 144,000, harvest.
  • Revelation 15-18: Bowls, Babylon falls.
  • Revelation 19: Return of Christ, marriage supper of the Lamb.
  • Revelation 20: Millennium, final judgment, great white throne.
  • Revelation 21-22: New heaven and new earth, no more death, river of life, "surely I come quickly," chapter 22:20.
  • Reading caution: Symbols are drawn from Daniel, Ezekiel, Isaiah, and Exodus. Answers should separate the image, the Old Testament source, and the interpretation, and flag where interpreters disagree on the millennium and the timing of events.

New Testament questions to be ready for

  • How do the four Gospels differ in audience and emphasis without contradicting on the core story?
  • What does justification by faith mean in Romans and Galatians, and how does James 2 fit?
  • What is the new covenant, and how does Hebrews use Jeremiah 31?
  • How should Revelation be read, literally, symbolically, or both, and where?
